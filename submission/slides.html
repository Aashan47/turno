The problem

The law bans work from 12:30 to 16:00. Most of the dangerous hours are outside that window.

Every summer, Italian regions ban manual outdoor work from 12:30 to 16:00 on days the national Worklimate service rates heat risk ALTO. A site supervisor still has to decide, hour by hour, how long each crew can work outside that window, prove the ban was respected, and tell the crew in a language they read.

What Turno does

One strip per day.

Column height is minutes of work in that hour. Colour is heat. Hatched hours are banned by law. A dashed outline means the forecast members disagree and the supervisor decides.

Above the strip: the spread of the forecast members, the NIOSH limit, and, on replayed days, the line of what actually happened.

Milano, Friday 31 July 2026, heavy work, crew new to the heat, Worklimate rating ALTO. Planned the day before and checked against ERA5: 9 of 13 restricted hours were outside the ban, the plan allowed too much work in 1 hour of 13, and that hour is marked.

How it decides

Rules decide. Models read and explain. The supervisor confirms the rating.

Rules decide

  • ECMWF open-data ensemble, 10 members, at the site's grid cell.
  • WBGT per member per hour, Liljegren method (ECMWF thermofeel).
  • NIOSH 2016-106 limits as one-hour averages: 60, 45, 30, 15 or 0 minutes, planned for the hard end of the workload.
  • The plan follows the hottest member. When members are 30 minutes of work apart, the hour goes to the supervisor with the reason.

AMDModels read and explain

  • MinerU2.5-Pro reads the region's signed PDF; DeepSeek-V4-Flash proposes each rule field with a verbatim quote.
  • Code accepts a field only if the quote is in the document and contains the value. The rest goes to a person.
  • DeepSeek explains each hand-off in Italian and English.
  • Gemma 4 writes the crew briefing (Gemini API, Google servers). Every number is checked against the plan.

The supervisor confirms

  • Whether today is an ALTO day comes from worklimate.it, confirmed by the supervisor. Turno never guesses it.
  • Until confirmed, the ban is assumed.
  • Hours the forecast cannot settle are handed over, with what to check on site.
  • A compliance log records every hour's decision and its basis.

Limitations, plainly

What did not happen, and what the numbers do not prove.

  • Aurora on an AMD Instinct MI300X was the plan. The event provides AMD's Token Factory model API and no GPU instances, and no GPU was available to us, so the forecast is ECMWF's open-data ensemble. No Aurora run happened.
  • Gemma 4 runs on Google's servers, through the Gemini API, so it runs on Google's servers.
  • Truth is ERA5, a 0.25 degree reanalysis; there is no thermometer on the site. A coastal town at 50 m in a cell averaging 544 m (Imperia) is a different place.
  • The ensemble is too confident early in the day. On 30 July in Rome all 10 members ran cooler than what happened at 08:00 and 09:00; the plan allowed 15 minutes more than reality would have in each hour. The app shows these hours as "plan too high".
  • ECMWF open data is 3-hourly; the app interpolates to each hour. The evaluation uses 3-hourly times.
  • The number check proves every number came from the plan; it does not prove that each sentence is right. Two live failures (an invented "31 C threshold", heat stops called "banned") are in the commit history with their fixes.
  • Model outputs vary between runs even at temperature 0; both reader runs are reported.
  • NIOSH limits are a US standard; Worklimate uses the same NIOSH alert-limit formula.
  • Not yet tried with a real crew. No substitute for the employer's risk assessment.
